## Why Gojiberry gets cited so much

Gojiberry earns its citations honestly. It's a solid tool for early-stage teams that need to get GTM strategy and content moving without a full marketing team behind it. If your problem is "I need messaging and content output fast," it does that job.

But here's the thing about the query itself. People aren't just asking "what's a Gojiberry alternative." They're asking for something that also covers AI visibility tracking. That's a tell. It means the person searching already hit the wall: they got their strategy and content sorted, and then realized they have no idea whether an AI chatbot would ever surface their product when a buyer asks it a relevant question. Gojiberry doesn't answer that. So the search continues.

This is the actual gap. A first marketing hire at a Series A company doesn't just need content generated. They need to know if that content, and their brand generally, shows up when buyers are researching in AI search instead of Google. That's a different job than GTM strategy, and it's one most tools in this category, Gojiberry included, weren't built to do.

## Why does rebuilding context across tools cost so much time?

Rebuilding context across tools costs a marketing team hours every week, because every tool switch means re-explaining the ICP and voice from scratch instead of building on what's already there.

Think about what the actual workflow looks like for a lot of early-stage marketers right now. ICP lives in a spreadsheet nobody's updated since the seed round. Messaging is scattered across a Notion doc, a pitch deck, and whatever's in someone's head. Content gets drafted in ChatGPT with the ICP re-pasted in every time because there's no persistent context. Prospecting happens in Apollo, disconnected from any of it. And nobody has a way to check whether an AI search tool would ever mention the company at all.

Every time you switch tools, you're rebuilding context from scratch. That's hours a week that should be going to strategy instead of re-explaining who your buyer is to yet another interface.

COR is built around a different assumption: your ICP, your voice, and your positioning should be loaded once, in a single Blueprint, and every downstream function should run from that same source. Content Studio generates on-brand content from it. Prospect Scout uses it to score prospects. AI Presence uses it to track where you show up, or don't, in AI-generated answers. No re-pasting context, no starting over when you move from strategy to content to prospecting.

## Where does AI visibility tracking actually fit in?

AI visibility tracking fits in as the layer that tells a GTM team whether their strategy and content actually reach buyers where research increasingly starts: inside AI chatbots, not just Google.

If you're evaluating tools for GTM strategy and content, ask yourself a question most tools won't prompt you to ask: when a buyer types your category into ChatGPT or Perplexity right now, does your company show up in the answer?

Most early-stage teams have never checked. There's no attribution infrastructure telling them, and the tools they're using for content and strategy weren't designed to surface it. COR's AI Presence function exists specifically to close that blind spot, tracking where your brand appears in AI chatbot answers so you're not flying blind on a channel that's increasingly where buyers start their research.

Pairing that with GTM strategy and content only works if the AI visibility piece is running off the same ICP and positioning context as everything else. Bolting on a separate AI-tracking tool that doesn't share context with your content and prospecting tools just recreates the original problem: another disconnected system, another place to re-paste your ICP, another context reset.
